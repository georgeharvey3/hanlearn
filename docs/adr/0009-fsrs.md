# 9. Schedule the reviews with FSRS

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-01
- Issue: #331
- Related: [0002-direction-level-scheduling.md](0002-direction-level-scheduling.md),
  [0007-grade-the-first-attempt.md](0007-grade-the-first-attempt.md),
  [0008-multiplicative-intervals.md](0008-multiplicative-intervals.md)

## Context

ADR 0008 gave each direction an interval and an ease. The interval grows when
the learner passes, and the ease is the multiplier. This is the SM-2 design that
Anki used, with the delay credit of the Anki v2 scheduler.

The ease is a heuristic. It is one number, and it holds no memory of when the
lapses happened. It cannot say how likely the learner is to recall a word today,
so the schedule cannot aim at a retention rate.

FSRS is a model of memory with two numbers for each card. The **stability** is
the number of days after which the recall probability falls to 0.9. The
**difficulty**, from 1 to 10, is how much one review moves the stability. The
model gives a forgetting curve, so the due date is the day on which the
predicted recall probability falls to the target retention.

On the open-spaced-repetition benchmark of 9,999 Anki collections and more than
350 million reviews, FSRS-6 has a mean log loss of 0.344. It beats SM-2 in 99.6%
of the collections. The simulation of that data implies that a learner needs 20%
to 30% fewer reviews for the same retention rate. The figure comes from logged
data, not from a trial with live learners.

## Decision

The app uses `ts-fsrs` for the schedule. Each direction carries a stability, a
difficulty, an interval and the date of its last review.

`ts-fsrs` is the TypeScript port of FSRS. It runs in the browser, it has no
dependencies, and it adds about 13 KB to the bundle after gzip. The app holds
one scheduler with three settings:

| Setting              | Value | Reason                                              |
| -------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| `request_retention`  | 0.9   | The target recall probability on the due date.      |
| `enable_short_term`  | false | The app has no intra-day step.                      |
| `enable_fuzz`        | false | `fuzzInterval` spreads the due date.                |

The weights are the FSRS-6 defaults. The app writes no review log, so it cannot
fit the weights to one learner. Per-user optimization needs a log, and a log
needs its own issue.

### The grades

The app has three grades and FSRS has four ratings.

| Grade   | Rating | Interval           |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------ |
| `pass`  | Good   | the day FSRS gives |
| `lapse` | Again  | the day FSRS gives |
| `fail`  | Again  | 0, so the next day |

A `lapse` is a wrong first attempt, so the graded attempt did not retrieve the
answer. Again is the rating for a failed retrieval, and it is what the typing
quiz of Anki sends. Hard means a retrieval that succeeded with difficulty, and
it grows the interval.

A lapse and a failure give the same memory state, and the due date separates
them. A learner who selected "I don't know" sees the direction the next day. A
learner who answered correctly on the second attempt sees it on the day that
FSRS names, which is one to three days for most directions.

The Easy rating has no button in this app, so it is not used.

### The state of a direction

| Field        | What it holds                                       |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| `stability`  | The days at which the recall probability is 0.9.    |
| `difficulty` | How much one review moves the stability, 1 to 10.   |
| `interval`   | The days from the last review to the next one.      |
| `lastReview` | The date of the last review.                        |
| `bank`       | The band of the interval, 1 to 5.                   |
| `dueDate`    | The interval plus the fuzz, from the last review.   |

The bank, the maximum interval of 365 days and the fuzz of up to 5% do not
change. ADR 0008 defines all three.

### The documents of the two calculations before this one

No migration runs. A direction gains the FSRS fields when a session next asks
it, and the read path seeds the memory state from what the document holds.

| The document holds     | Stability          | Difficulty          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
| A stability            | The stored value   | The stored value    |
| An interval and an ease| The interval       | From the ease       |
| A bank only            | The seed interval  | From the start ease |

The stability of an interval is the interval itself. Both are the number of days
at which the recall probability is 0.9, so a seeded direction keeps the schedule
that it had.

The ease and the difficulty measure the same thing in opposite directions, and
the map between them is linear across the range of each. The ease range is 1.3
to 3.0, and the difficulty range is 10 to 1. The start ease of 2.5 gives a
difficulty of about 3.6.

A direction at bank 1 has a stability of 0. FSRS reads it as a new card, and the
first question that passes it gives it its first stability and difficulty.

The app stops writing the ease. A direction that holds one keeps it until its
next review.

## Consequences

Good:

- The due date comes from a model of the memory of the learner, and the model
  aims at a retention rate that the app can state and change.
- A late correct review is evidence of a stable memory, and the model reads that
  evidence itself. ADR 0008 approximated it with half of the delay.
- The intervals grow faster for a word that the learner knows. A direction at 30
  days that passes on time goes to 97 days, where the old calculation gave 75.
- The state is the state that the FSRS optimizer needs. An issue that adds a
  review log can fit the weights to one learner.

Bad:

- A lapse is a much larger penalty than before. A direction at 30 days that
  lapses comes back in 2 days, where the old calculation gave 15. The learner
  sees a word that they got wrong far more often.
- The default weights are the mean of many collections, and they are not fitted
  to this learner or to Chinese vocabulary.
- The dashboard moves. A direction reaches bank 5 in four passes, and the level
  distribution rises for one or two sessions after the release.
- The app depends on a third-party library for the core of its schedule. A
  change in the FSRS weights between releases of `ts-fsrs` changes every due
  date that follows it.
- The tests hold the numbers that the default weights give. A release of
  `ts-fsrs` that changes the weights fails them.

## Alternatives

**Write the FSRS-6 formulas as pure functions in `scheduling.ts`.** The math is
about 100 lines, and it adds nothing to the bundle. The 21 weights are then hand
copied, and every correction upstream is a manual port. The library is the safer
of the two for a model this app does not own.

**Send a lapse to the Hard rating.** Hard keeps three memory outcomes, one for
each grade. FSRS reads Hard as a success, so a lapse then grows the interval: a
direction at 30 days goes to 71 days after a wrong first attempt. This
contradicts the meaning that ADR 0008 gives a lapse.

**Give a failure the day that FSRS names.** The model says that a direction with
a stability of 7 days is best asked in 7 days, even after a failure. The app
asks it the next day, because a learner who selected "I don't know" has not
learned the word, and the Learn step is where they learn it. This is the one
place where the app overrides the model.

**Set the target retention to 0.85.** A lower target gives fewer reviews for a
lower retention rate. 0.9 is the default of `ts-fsrs`, and it is the top of the
range in the issue. A later issue can move it, or give it to the learner, when
there is data on the workload that it gives.
